## State (main, end of 2026-10-26)

The v1.0 foundation (BACKLOG F2-F20 minus F1/F5) is built and verified on Linux and Windows:

- **World:** seeded 40 x 40 km city (CityGen), 229 settlements, chunk streaming, building
  collision; day/night at 48:1 (clocks only; everyone walks at real-time speed).
- **Population:** 1,000,000 coarse agents on a 1 Hz background tick (integer, deterministic);
  full NPCs near players (budget 600 shared, nearest people win, 20 m pop-in clearance for
  people not already shown); crowd placeholders (pills) out to 200 m with velocities; people
  indoors at night; bodies lie 90 s; births reuse unreferenced dead slots; the dead drift in
  from just outside the city; encounter odds scale with population (about 1%/game day).
- **Combat and defeat:** melee and pistol, infection and cure, capture and rescue, turned
  players as named zombies, permadeath setting, ledgered item transfers, Virtual Game Master
  opportunities.
- **Knowledge:** facts become rumors spread by contact (64 slots); H asks an NPC, I inspects one.
- **Economy (F19/F19b):** four goods per settlement, profiles, daily luck, faction logistics,
  3-6 day stocks, rationing, hardships, refugees, prices, great hordes that warn first and
  overrun crucial settlements (stores destroyed, output crippled, rebuilt with materials).
- **Factions (F20):** relations grid; one move per faction per game day (faction-scale trade,
  raid, truce, hold); temperaments stand in for leaders.
- **Visibility:** M opens the full-transparency debug map (settlements, shortages, order,
  relations, last moves). `scripts/inspect_world.py` summarises a save.
- **Formats:** save v5 (v1-v4 migrate with byte-exact backups), protocol 8 (server and every
  client must match), content `questsim:city-v1`, data-only mods.
- **Tests:** 157 domain checks; ENet suites (items, build, multiplayer, save failures, defeat,
  content, living); Xvfb screen tests (Linux only); E1 CPU/Vulkan bit-identity; 8-player
  1M-agent load test; a 40-day economy soak (`scripts/run_soak.sh`). `test_all.sh --all` runs
  everything (~10 minutes here, ~13 on whitetop). The GitHub workflow is manual-only.

Measured: 1M agents at 20 ticks/s with 8 players, about 93 KiB/s per player; the coarse step
is about 0.06 s here and 0.12 s on whitetop (budget 1 s), 0.4 s there with a rumor spreading.

## Lessons worth keeping

- The soak finds design flaws tests do not: road-hop sharing too slow, no surplus means no
  trade, thresholds tuned at the edge of noise fade out, map-edge arrivals never arrive,
  density-dependent lethality (20%/day at 1M vs 2% at 100k). Measure at the real scale.
- Windows: `File.Move` fails while another process reads the target (use
  `WorldStorage.ReplaceFile`); `python3` is the Store stub (`test_all.sh` picks `python`);
  `*.sh` must stay LF (`.gitattributes`); suspend processes with `pause()` in
  verify_multiplayer.py, not SIGSTOP.
- Keep population code integer-only (no Math.Cos): CPU, GPU and both platforms must replay
  the same history.
- Watch per-client bandwidth when adding snapshot data: the settlement table once cost every
  client 24 KB/s because the world JSON it rode in changes every second.

## Road to v1.0 (proposal, for discussion)

v1.0 per the director: a foundation to build on, with a living world (humans, zombies,
factions, rumors), rudimentary procedural generation, basic RPG stats, gunplay and melee.
Most systems exist; what is missing is that they reach the player, plus the plumbing to
ship. Proposed milestones, each a few bounded tasks:

1. **Close the depth loop** (BACKLOG F21, F22, F18, F23): caravans and raid parties as
   travelling groups players can meet; leaders with traits and permanent succession; danger
   zones from order; Game Master opportunities from economy and faction events (escort,
   defend against a great horde, deliver, intercept); buying and selling at scarcity prices.
   This is where the simulation becomes gameplay.
2. **A session's worth of play:** a minimal inventory and trade UI, looting bodies and caches,
   a player-facing map (rumor-driven, unlike the debug map), clear feedback for defeat,
   infection and opportunities. Small, readable, placeholder art.
3. **Building v1 (F15):** grid walls, doors, a container, placed anywhere valid with material
   costs and demolition; NPCs and hordes path around them. Ties into materials and rebuilding.
4. **Shipping hygiene:** Godot export builds for Linux and Windows (never verified), a
   dedicated-server package and config file, real LAN and WAN sessions with people, save
   rotation and backups, a power-loss durability test, a written compatibility policy for
   saves, protocol and mods, and a short player/host README.
5. **Scale and hardware check:** declare a minimum spec (whitetop is a good candidate) and
   hold 20 ticks/s there with 8 players; revisit 16 players. GPU population (F1/F5) only if
   the measurements ask for it.

**v1.0 gate (proposal):** the DIRECTOR.md "Weekly review" as a scripted plus human
playtest on export builds, two machines over a network: inspect faction wants, let the world
act, make a material change with one player while the other is away, find the resulting
opportunity, build at a doorway, reconnect and reload, and explain what happened without
developer logs. Plus test_all --all green on both platforms.

Suggested order: 1 then 2 (they feed each other), 4 started early in parallel because
exports are unverified, then 3, then 5 as the gate approaches.
