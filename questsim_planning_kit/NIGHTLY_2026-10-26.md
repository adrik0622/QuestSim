## Decisions the director must make (morning review)

Each entry gives what the agent chose for now; all are easy to change.

1. **What a turned player becomes.** Now implemented your idea: an existing coarse zombie is
   "possessed" at the spot, titled "(once <name>)", carries the player's gear and roams with its
   horde; a "put them to rest" opportunity tracks it; killing it recovers the gear and pays once.
   If anything else kills it, the gear drops as a remains cache. Without a population (or with
   permadeath) the gear drops as a remains cache. The profile starts a new character.
2. **Defeat timings.** Chosen: infection turns after 20 online minutes; captivity lasts 3
   online minutes; injury 5 online minutes (−4 melee damage). Tune freely.
3. **Where captives are held.** Chosen: where they fell (a real location). A camp inside the
   Choir's own settlement would be more thematic.
4. **Friendly fire.** Chosen: off; attacks only hit NPCs. PvP would need a decision.
5. **Faction standing numbers.** Chosen: −25 per member killed, hostile at −50, Union starts
   friendly (+20), the Choir is always hostile. Is co-op standing shared by all players
   (chosen) or per player?
9. **Detail budget.** Chosen: 400 detailed NPCs in total, shared nearest-first round robin
   across players (fair for spread-out groups). Raise/lower with hardware?
10. **Your existing play save.** Verified on a copy: your v1 save (3 characters) migrates to
   v4 with a byte-exact backup, but stays unpopulated. To bring it into the living city, start
   the server once with `--populate=1000000` (tested on the copy). Or start fresh with a new
   `--save` path. The original file was never modified tonight.
7. **CI minutes.** A GitHub Actions workflow runs on pushes to main/nightly and PRs (Linux +
   Windows domain checks, population CPU checks, game compile; a few minutes per push). A
   heavier `game-suites` job (real ENet suites with pinned Godot) is **manual only** and has
   not been run yet. Free for public repos; metered for private ones. Keep, trim or disable?
8. **Mod trust tier.** Mods are data-only (items, starting kits). Code mods (scripts) are
   not supported; that needs a trust/sandbox decision before any work.
6. **Population snapshot cadence.** Chosen: every 60 s during play plus at shutdown; a crash
   loses at most that much NPC movement and rumor spread (never items, deaths or facts).

## Decisions the agent made (reversible)

- **Order:** F3 world → F2/F5 population in the server with detail tiers → F7 stats and
  combat → F10 defeat and Virtual Game Master → F8 facts/hearing → F9 faction loop → F4
  saves at scale → F14 CI. F1 (locality) is deferred: 10⁶ agents fit the CPU path.
- **World:** 40 × 40 km, game coordinates in metres centred on the spawn room. City radius
  ~5 km with a street grid (120 m block pitch); 8 outskirt towns 13–17 km out, connected by
  straight roads; rural tiles elsewhere (field or forest). Integer-only generation, so
  client and server regenerate identical chunks from the seed + generator version; only edits
  are stored. Chunks are 128 m.
- **Spawn:** the existing Q01/Q02 room becomes an enclosed building in the central plaza
  (perimeter walls added, door on the south side), so Q01/Q02 tests keep their meaning.
- **Population in the game:** a CPU tick (the E1 reference, adapted) every real second on a
  background task; the authority only touches population arrays between ticks. Agents near
  players are promoted to 20 Hz detail and written back when players leave. GPU backend later.
- **Population mix:** 1,000,000 agents by default (configurable); 75% human (3 factions,
  one of them hostile), 25% zombies in hordes; no horde attractors in the spawn district.
